# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Mostly people who arrive from Bríd's LinkedIn profile: recruiters, colleagues, people Bríd has just met. They're on a laptop or a phone, have a couple of minutes, and want to get a sense of Bríd and what Bríd makes. Friends and family who the apps were built for are a secondary audience.

## Product Purpose

A home for Bríd O'Donnell's side projects, replacing a one-line page from 2023. It succeeds when a visitor comes away knowing what Bríd has built and why, with a sense of the person rather than a CV.

## Positioning

Bríd builds little apps for the people Bríd cares about: things they can enjoy, or that make their lives easier. Every project on the site is introduced by who it was for and what it fixed or made fun.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary action: open a project and look at what it does. Secondary: connect on LinkedIn (https://www.linkedin.com/in/brid-o-donnell/).
- The line a visitor remembers: Bríd makes small, thoughtful apps for the people around them.
- Belief ladder: these are real, finished things people use → each one came from noticing a specific person's problem → Bríd is curious about a wide range of things → worth getting to know.
- Proof on hand: the projects themselves (IMC Seat Tracker, Plant Tracker, May Madness, Priorities, Ballpark, Budget), with screenshots.

## Brand Personality

Curious about everything, and caring. Interests range across film, money, mental arithmetic, fashion and party games, and the site should feel like that range belongs to one person. Warm and personal in voice: first person, specific, a little funny, never self-promotional.

## Anti-references

A corporate CV: job titles, bullet-point achievements, a circular headshot, LinkedIn transplanted onto a webpage.

## Design Principles

1. **Who it was for, before what it does.** Each project leads with the person and the problem, then the tech.
2. **Show the thing.** Real screenshots of the apps carry the page; no decorative filler standing in for work.
3. **Let the range show.** Projects can each have their own visual world, held together by a consistent voice.
4. **Small and finished over big and vague.** Specific details (the typo it catches, the cinema it tracks) beat general claims.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No specific requirements given. Readable contrast, keyboard access, and reduced-motion alternatives as a baseline.
